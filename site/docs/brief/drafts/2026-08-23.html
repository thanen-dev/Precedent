◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia is navigating a compressed policy window in which three structural deadlines are converging: the 2029 EU EBA termination, the LDC graduation pressure that will strip additional trade preferences, and the Funan Techo Canal's ongoing construction which is deepening Chinese infrastructure dependency at precisely the moment Western market access is most at risk. The Hun Manet government's Pentagonal Strategy provides rhetorical coherence, but implementation authority is fragmented across technocratic ministries whose incentives diverge sharply from the patronage logic still operating at the political apex. The macro-fiscal position remains stable under Pornmoniroth's stewardship, but that stability is underwritten by preference-dependent export revenues that face systematic erosion before 2030.

This is not a technical coordination failure — it is a structural conflict between the faction of the government that needs Western market access to survive economically and the faction that extracts rents from the deforestation and land concession economy. If the EU accelerates its EBA review timeline on environmental grounds — which the Sri Lanka parallel (82% match) suggests is a real risk — Nimul and Siphana will face the choice of publicly acknowledging regulatory non-compliance or defending a record they did not produce. That choice will signal whether technocratic reformers have any effective authority over patronage extraction or merely provide diplomatic cover for it.

Factories operating on thin margins through regulatory arbitrage exit or consolidate under larger players. The net employment effect is negative in the short term — 50,000–150,000 job losses in a compressed 24–36 month window — before stabilising as compliant factories capture a larger share of orders from brands that can no longer absorb reputational risk from non-compliant supply chains. For Cambodia, the critical variable the Bangladesh case does not resolve is the EBA termination date: Bangladesh's preference erosion was gradual and partially offset by GSP mechanisms, whereas Cambodia's 2029 EU EBA deadline is a hard legal event, not a negotiated glide path. This makes the downside scenario steeper and faster than the Bangladesh experience, particularly if Nimul cannot secure an interim arrangement that rewards compliance progress before the deadline triggers.
