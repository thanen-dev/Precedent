◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia enters this week under compounding structural pressure: the 2029 EBA deadline is now inside a three-year horizon, making it operationally urgent rather than diplomatically deferrable, while the Funan Techo Canal project continues to deepen Chinese infrastructure dependency at precisely the moment Western market access is most at risk. Hun Manet's government is maintaining macroeconomic stability—Pornmoniroth's fiscal discipline is holding—but the underlying growth model faces a legitimacy crisis: garment-led export revenue is threatened on both the demand side (EBA) and the supply side (Vietnamese and Bangladeshi price competition). The Pentagonal Strategy's long-horizon framing masks a short-term coherence problem: the ministers responsible for the future (Naron on human capital, Vandeth on digital, Serey on monetary sovereignty) are operating on decade-plus timelines while the ministers managing current exposure (Nimul on trade, Chanthol on FDI) face decisions that close in 30 months.

⚠ CONFLICT ALERT

**The EBA-Canal Contradiction: The Single Most Dangerous Policy Incoherence in the Current Government.**

The Funan Techo Canal deepens Cambodia's Chinese infrastructure dependency in a manner that is directly legible to EU conditionality assessors as a democratic governance and geopolitical alignment signal—not merely an infrastructure choice. Nimul's EBA preservation strategy requires demonstrating reform credibility to Brussels. Say Sam Al's Ministry of Environment approved canal-adjacent concessions with minimal EBA-compliant environmental review. Chanthol co-chairs the China-Cambodia Intergovernmental Coordination Committee, the institutional vehicle managing the canal relationship. These three officials are pulling in structurally incompatible directions: Nimul needs Brussels to see reform momentum; Chanthol needs Beijing to see strategic partnership continuity; Say Sam Al's patronage incentives require approving the extraction concessions that make the canal economically viable for CPP-connected interests. The policy coherence consequence is that Cambodia cannot simultaneously signal to the EU that it is governing in good faith and signal to China that the canal is an unconditional strategic commitment. Hun Manet has not resolved this contradiction—he has deferred it. With the EBA deadline now inside 36 months, deferral is itself a policy choice with compounding costs.

◈ HISTORICAL PARALLEL

**Sri Lanka 2010–2017 (82% match, Funan-Canal/Infrastructure-Dependency Signal) is the strongest and most actionable parallel this week.**

The Rajapaksa-era infrastructure logic in Sri Lanka is Cambodia's closest structural twin: Chinese-financed megaprojects approved through relationship channels rather than institutional review, environmental and fiscal risk assessment subordinated to political timeline, opposition framed as anti-development rather than engaged as legitimate scrutiny, and Western market access maintained in parallel as a separate track that policymakers believed could coexist indefinitely with Chinese infrastructure dependency. The Sri Lanka case predicts the following trajectory for Cambodia: the coexistence of Chinese infrastructure dependency and Western market access will hold until a fiscal shock or a Western conditionality trigger forces a binary choice, at which point the institutional capacity to pivot will be insufficient because the relationship-based governance model has hollowed out the agencies that would need to manage the adjustment. Cambodia's differentiating factor—and it is a real one—is that Pornmoniroth's fiscal discipline is meaningfully stronger than Sri Lanka's under Rajapaksa, and Serey's NBC is a more functional institution than the Central Bank of Sri Lanka was in 2015. These differences delay the Sri Lanka scenario; they do not prevent it if EBA revenues collapse and the fiscal buffer is consumed.
