◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia enters this week in a structurally precarious position: the 2029 EBA deadline is now under 30 months away, the Funan Techo Canal remains a fiscal and diplomatic flashpoint, and the leadership coalition is running three partially incompatible growth strategies simultaneously. Hun Manet's government is holding together through patronage continuity inherited from Hun Sen rather than through policy coherence, while the technocratic cluster — Pornmoniroth, Serey, Chanthol — produces credible economic management that masks deeper structural fragilities. The macro numbers remain defensible (5–6% growth, stable riel, manageable debt service) but the composition of growth has not shifted materially toward the diversification targets embedded in the Pentagonal Strategy, and the external shock window — US tariff exposure, EU conditionality, China infrastructure dependency — is narrowing.

⚠ CONFLICT ALERT

The single most dangerous internal contradiction is between **Say Sam Al's extractive concession approval pipeline and Cham Nimul's EBA compliance roadmap**. Nimul's 2029 deadline is legally binding — EU EBA termination is not a negotiating position, it is a scheduled legal event — and her trade-through-rules doctrine requires demonstrable progress on labour rights, governance, and environmental standards. Say Sam Al's ministry is simultaneously approving concessions that generate the deforestation and displacement evidence that EU monitoring bodies use to benchmark Cambodia's compliance trajectory. These two officials report to the same cabinet and their policy outputs are in direct operational conflict. The consequence is not merely diplomatic embarrassment: each concession approval that generates international NGO documentation weakens Nimul's position in Brussels and reduces the probability of a successful EBA negotiation outcome. With 700,000 garment jobs at stake, this is not an abstract governance contradiction — it is a material economic risk. Pornmoniroth's fiscal stability model depends on those export revenues, and Chanthol's FDI attraction pitch depends on maintaining EU market access as a credential. The concession pipeline is quietly undermining three of the most competent technocrats in the cabinet simultaneously.

◈ HISTORICAL PARALLEL

**Sri Lanka 2010–2022 (Funan Techo Canal / infrastructure dependency signal, 82% match)** is the strongest predictive parallel for Cambodia's current trajectory. The Sri Lanka case demonstrates how Chinese infrastructure financing, initially absorbed as a confidence signal and growth accelerant, compounds into a sovereignty constraint when debt service obligations coincide with an external revenue shock — in Sri Lanka's case, tourism collapse and import bill expansion; in Cambodia's case, the potential scenario is EBA loss plus garment sector contraction plus canal cost overruns arriving simultaneously in the 2028–2031 window. The Sri Lanka parallel predicts: (1) the infrastructure commitment will be completed regardless of changed fiscal conditions because political face is attached to it; (2) the debt service terms will not be publicly disclosed until they become undeniable; (3) the technocratic finance ministry will be aware of the risk but lack the political standing to halt the project; and (4) the crisis, if it arrives, will be presented domestically as an external shock rather than a consequence of financing decisions. Pornmoniroth is structurally in the position of Sri Lanka's pre-crisis finance technocrats — competent, aware, and institutionally constrained from acting on what he knows. The 82% match is not a prediction of collapse; it is a prediction of compressing fiscal headroom in the 2028–2032 window, with the severity determined by whether EBA is retained and whether canal construction costs remain within disclosed parameters.
