◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia enters the second half of 2026 navigating a narrowing economic corridor: the 2029 EBA deadline is now inside a three-year window, the Funan Techo Canal remains a fiscal and diplomatic flashpoint, and post-COVID growth momentum is decelerating toward the 5–6% range that Hem Vanndy and Aun Pornmoniroth have both publicly anchored as the near-term ceiling. The Pentagonal Strategy provides rhetorical coherence, but implementation authority is fragmented across at least five ministries with divergent doctrinal priorities. Hun Manet's government is structurally stable but operationally contested: the inherited CPP patronage architecture that Hun Sen constructed over 38 years constrains precisely the institutional reforms that Cambodia's multilateral partners—and its own technocrats—regard as prerequisites for LDC graduation.

◈ DOCTRINE WATCH

**1. Chea Serey's de-dollarisation acceleration.** The NBC's cross-border QR payment expansion (Japan Phase 1 now live; Singapore and India in pipeline) is the most consequential structural shift in Cambodian monetary architecture since the 1993 liberalisation. Serey is building a riel-denominated transaction layer beneath a still-dollarised economy. The doctrine signal: financial sovereignty is being pursued incrementally and institutionally, specifically to avoid the shock-therapy vulnerability that Argentina's parallel currency experiments produced. Watch whether the MEF under Pornmoniroth formally endorses riel-lane fiscal disbursement—that would mark a phase transition from NBC-led experiment to whole-of-government policy.

**2. Sun Chanthol's tariff-reduction offer to Washington as multi-vector doctrine.** Chanthol's 2025 offer to cut the Cambodia-US tariff differential by 19 percentage points was not a trade concession—it was a strategic signal: Cambodia is the willing alternative-to-China manufacturing platform for US-aligned supply chain reshoring. This doctrine is now the leading edge of Cambodia's foreign economic policy and it runs in direct tension with Hun Sen's legacy China dependency architecture and the Funan Canal's deepening infrastructure entanglement with Beijing. The doctrine is Chanthol's; whether Hun Manet will operationalise it fully is the open question.

**3. Sok Siphana and Cham Nimul's LDC graduation legal framework race.** With 2029 now inside a single electoral cycle, the Cambodia Trade Integration Strategy framework that Siphana chairs and Nimul executes is shifting from long-range planning to crisis-mode sequencing. The doctrine emerging from this pair: rules-based integration as irreversible reform lock-in—use international legal deadlines to force domestic vested interests to accept changes they would otherwise block. This is the most sophisticated institutional judo in the cabinet, and it is operating largely below political visibility.

⚠ CONFLICT ALERT

The single most dangerous internal contradiction is the **structural incompatibility between Say Sam Al's concession-approval pipeline and Sun Chanthol's FDI diversification doctrine.** Chanthol's pitch to mid-tier US, Japanese, and European manufacturers explicitly rests on environmental compliance signals—ISO membership, SEZ standards, ASEAN supply chain credibility. Say Sam Al's ministry is simultaneously approving concessions that generate the deforestation and land-rights violations that are documented by EU due diligence mechanisms and directly cited in EBA conditionality reviews. These two actors are not in dialogue; they operate in separate patronage lanes. The consequence for policy coherence is acute: every environmental violation that Chanthol's target investors can Google undermines the compliance credibility that Chanthol, Nimul, and Siphana are collectively constructing through multilateral engagement. If the EU's 2026–2027 EBA review process formally elevates environmental governance as a conditionality trigger—a plausible escalation given the EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive's extraterritorial reach—the cabinet will face a direct choice between the patronage revenue that Say Sam Al's approval pipeline generates and the $6B+ in annual EBA-linked garment export value. Hun Manet does not currently have the political capital to resolve this contradiction through institutional override.

◈ HISTORICAL PARALLEL

**Sri Lanka 2010–2022 (82% match, Funan Techo Canal signal).** The strongest structural twin this week is not the Bangladesh garment parallel (which is sector-specific) but the Sri Lanka infrastructure dependency arc. Rajapaksa-era Sri Lanka accepted Chinese infrastructure financing at terms that were commercially rational in isolation but created systemic debt-service obligations that crowded out the fiscal space needed to respond to external shocks. Cambodia's Funan Techo Canal—a $1.7B project with Chinese financing architecture that has not been publicly stress-tested against commodity export scenarios—replicates the Hambantota structural logic precisely: strategic infrastructure with genuine development rationale, financed at terms that embed political obligations, located in a country whose fiscal buffers (Pornmoniroth's domain) are adequate in baseline scenarios but thin under stress. The Sri Lanka case predicts: the risk does not materialise as a financing crisis in years one through five; it materialises as a policy constraint in years six through ten, when debt-service obligations compress the government's ability to respond to an external demand shock (EBA loss, US tariff escalation, garment sector displacement). Pornmoniroth's fiscal-first doctrine is the correct institutional defense, but it requires that he have visibility into the Canal's true debt-service schedule—and there is no public evidence that this transparency exists within the MEF.
