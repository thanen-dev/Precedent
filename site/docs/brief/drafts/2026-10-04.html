◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia is entering a structurally vulnerable phase in which its three primary economic lifelines—garment exports under EBA preferences, Chinese infrastructure financing, and FDI inflows—are simultaneously under pressure from external shocks and internal governance deficits. Hun Manet's government retains nominal political stability through the inherited CPP patronage architecture, but the reform coalition needed to navigate LDC graduation by 2027 is functionally fragmented across ministries with incompatible time horizons and risk tolerances. The macro-fiscal picture remains stable on headline indicators (5–6% projected growth), but this masks a structural diversification failure: Cambodia is three years from EBA termination with no credible replacement market access framework in place.

⚠ CONFLICT ALERT

The single most dangerous internal contradiction is the **structural conflict between Say Sam Al's environment ministry (extraction-as-development, short time horizon, patronage-driven concession approval) and Sok Siphana/Nimul's trade rules architecture (long-horizon, multilateral compliance, EU governance conditionality)**. Cambodia's EBA retention is conditioned in part on environmental and land rights governance standards—the same standards that Say Sam Al's concession approval process systematically violates. This is not a bureaucratic turf dispute: it is a direct policy coherence failure in which one ministry is actively destroying the conditions that another ministry requires to preserve market access worth an estimated $5–7 billion annually in garment export preferences. Pornmoniroth's MEF sits between these forces but has no enforcement authority over the environment ministry. Hun Manet has not resolved this contradiction because Say Sam Al's patron network (Supreme Court Justice Dith Munty, brother at NBC) is precisely the kind of relationship infrastructure the CPP cannot afford to alienate. The implication: Cambodia's EBA negotiating position is being weakened from inside the government in real time.

◈ HISTORICAL PARALLEL

**Strongest match: Sri Lanka 2010–2022 (82% confidence, Funan Techo Canal/infrastructure dependency signal).**

The parallel is more precise than the match score suggests. Sri Lanka under Rajapaksa deepened Chinese infrastructure financing (Hambantota, Colombo Port City) while maintaining the appearance of export competitiveness and fiscal stability—until the external shock (commodity price shift + COVID) revealed that the infrastructure had not generated the productive capacity needed to service the debt. Cambodia's Funan Techo Canal is structurally analogous: large-scale Chinese-financed infrastructure whose economic returns are uncertain, whose geopolitical costs (reduced Mekong water flow, Vietnamese trade friction) are real and near-term, and whose financing terms are opaque to the multilateral partners Cambodia needs for EBA retention and World Bank budget support. The Sri Lanka case predicts a 4–6 year lag between the infrastructure commitment and the fiscal stress event—placing Cambodia's vulnerability window at approximately 2028–2031, precisely when EBA termination and LDC graduation pressures peak simultaneously. The escape path Sri Lanka lacked and Cambodia still has: a functioning technocratic reform coalition (Serey, Pornmoniroth, Naron, Siphana) with genuine multilateral credibility. Whether Hun Manet allows that coalition to operate with sufficient autonomy is the decisive variable.
