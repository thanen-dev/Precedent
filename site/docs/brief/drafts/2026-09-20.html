◈ SITUATION ASSESSMENT

Cambodia enters this week in a structural squeeze between its two most binding external deadlines: the 2029 EU EBA termination and the progressive loss of US tariff preferences that Chanthol's 2025 negotiations only partially arrested. GDP growth projections from Vanndy's industrial planning conference (5.0–5.2% range) remain credible in the near term but are built on a garment sector that faces simultaneous labour-standard pressure and price competition from Vietnam and Bangladesh—a combination the Bangladesh twin match rates at 91% probability of forcing painful structural adjustment. Domestically, the transition from Hun Sen's relationship-based political economy to Hun Manet's nominally more institutionalized governance remains incomplete, creating a dual-power environment in which the Senate presidency functions as a shadow veto mechanism over decisions that threaten legacy patronage flows.

◈ DOCTRINE WATCH

**1. Chea Serey's De-Dollarisation Acceleration.** The NBC governor's cross-border Bakong QR expansion—now spanning Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Malaysia, South Korea, Japan, and the Alipay+ network—has moved from pilot to operational infrastructure faster than most analysts projected. The doctrine signal here is financial sovereignty-through-infrastructure: Serey is creating riel-denominated transaction corridors that reduce USD dependency at the settlement layer before attempting to change household savings behavior. Watch whether she moves to mandate Bakong settlement for SEZ payroll transactions—that would be the first coercive de-dollarization step and a significant escalation from voluntary adoption.

**2. Chanthol's "Platform State" Pitch Under Tariff Stress.** Chanthol's FDI promotion doctrine—Cambodia as the easiest manufacturing platform regardless of investor origin—is under stress from the residual US tariff overhang. His 2025 offer to cut Cambodia's tariffs by 19 percentage points bought partial relief but did not fully resolve the competitive disadvantage against Vietnam. The doctrine question this week is whether CDC's investment pipeline data shows Chinese manufacturers continuing to route through Cambodian SEZs despite scrutiny, or whether nearshoring interest from non-Chinese manufacturers is actually materializing. If the pipeline is still predominantly Chinese-origin FDI, Chanthol's "multi-vector" positioning claim is a diplomatic narrative rather than an economic reality.

**3. Nimul's Rules-Based Trade Framing Under LDC Graduation Pressure.** With 2029 now under three years away, Cham Nimul's WTO-anchored doctrine—that multilateral rules protect small economies from bilateral coercion—is entering its hardest test. The EBA termination is not a sanction; it is a legal graduation event, and Nimul's ministry must now operationalize the Cambodia Trade Integration Strategy in sectors (electronics, agro-processing, footwear) that are not yet export-ready. The doctrine signal to watch is whether Nimul begins advocating for bilateral FTA negotiations as a bridge, which would represent a quiet retreat from pure multilateralism and a significant shift in Cambodia's stated trade doctrine.

⚠ CONFLICT ALERT

The single most dangerous internal contradiction is the **direct collision between Say Sam Al's resource-extraction environmental doctrine and the EBA compliance requirements that Cham Nimul and Aun Pornmoniroth need to preserve market access through 2029.**

EU EBA conditionality includes environmental governance standards. Say Sam Al's ministry has functioned as a concession-permitting body with de minimis enforcement, and his family's political ties (son-in-law of Supreme Court Justice Dith Munty, brother of NBC Deputy Governor Say Samath) make him structurally insulated from accountability pressure. The contradiction is not merely bureaucratic: Pornmoniroth's fiscal stability model depends on export revenue that flows from EBA-preserved garment sector access; Nimul's trade doctrine depends on demonstrating rule-of-law compliance to Brussels; but Say Sam Al's patronage obligations require continued concession approvals that generate the deforestation and land-clearing records that EU parliamentary delegations cite in EBA review hearings. No one in cabinet has the political standing to force Say Sam Al to implement genuine enforcement, because doing so attacks a CPP patronage revenue stream that predates Hun Manet's premiership and has Hun Sen's implicit protection. This means Cambodia will attempt to manage EBA compliance through cosmetic reporting rather than operational reform—a strategy that becomes increasingly untenable as Brussels moves toward satellite-based deforestation monitoring that bypasses Cambodian self-reporting entirely.

◈ HISTORICAL PARALLEL

**Sri Lanka 2010–2022 (Funan Techo Canal / Infrastructure Dependency — 82% match).**

The parallel is not the debt trap narrative in its crude form—Cambodia has not borrowed at the volume that collapsed Colombo. The structural match is more precise: a government that used Chinese infrastructure financing to signal development ambition and shore up domestic political legitimacy, while systematically underinvesting in the export diversification and institutional reforms that would have reduced external vulnerability. Sri Lanka's Rajapaksa governments (2010–2019) ran exactly this playbook—Hambantota, Mattala airport, Lotus Tower—while GSP+ trade preference compliance deteriorated and foreign exchange reserves were consumed maintaining an overvalued exchange rate. The collapse came when the external shock (COVID tourism loss, fertilizer crisis) hit a structurally weakened balance of payments with no IMF relationship and no diversified export base as buffer.

Cambodia's Funan Techo Canal occupies the same political-economic role as Hambantota: domestically legitimizing, strategically dependent, and diverting fiscal attention and political capital from the harder work of export diversification. The predictive implication is not imminent collapse—Cambodia's dollarized economy and NBC reserves provide more short-term buffer than Colombo had—but a narrowing window. If EBA preferences are lost on schedule in 2029 without a materially different export basket, and if the Canal construction absorbs the political bandwidth that should be going to Nimul's trade diversification agenda, Cambodia reaches 2030 with Chinese infrastructure dependency deepened, Western market access reduced, and no institutional reform record sufficient to negotiate the multilateral support package that Pornmoniroth would need in a balance-of-payments stress scenario.
